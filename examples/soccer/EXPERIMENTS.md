# Coastal/Burnaby experiments

The recommended experiment is a general COCO person detector followed by the
uniform-reference classifier. It separates finding people from deciding their
team or role. The older soccer detector and all existing modes remain available.

## Run the current pipeline

From the repository root, after installing `requirements-local.txt`:

```bash
export MPLCONFIGDIR="$PWD/.cache/matplotlib"
export YOLO_CONFIG_DIR="$PWD/.cache/ultralytics"
export HF_HOME="$PWD/.cache/huggingface"
curl -fL -o examples/soccer/data/yolov8m.pt \
  https://github.com/ultralytics/assets/releases/download/v8.3.0/yolov8m.pt
.venv/bin/python examples/soccer/main.py \
  --source_video_path examples/soccer/data/coastal-vs-burnaby-90m00s-90m05s.mp4 \
  --target_video_path examples/soccer/output/current-uniforms.mp4 \
  --mode UNIFORM_CLASSIFICATION --device cpu --no_display \
  --person_model examples/soccer/data/yolov8m.pt \
  --uniform_config examples/soccer/data/uniform-references/config.json \
  --analysis_path examples/soccer/output/current-uniforms.jsonl
```

Weights and current reference images already exist in this local checkout; skip
the download when using them. The local references cover blue and black players,
a yellow referee, and red and green goalkeepers. Optional `color` fields choose
annotation colours. Integer track IDs are generated by the tracker, not read
from jersey numbers. A person detection is not itself evidence that someone is
a player: spectators may also be detected and resemble a team's uniform.

`--person_model` works in `PLAYER_DETECTION` and `UNIFORM_CLASSIFICATION`. The
model must contain a class named `person`. The adapter preserves person boxes
and confidence; uniform roles are determined separately. Other modes retain
their original detectors.

## Evidence and limitations

- Twelve frames across 05:00, 20:00, 40:00 and 60:00: general inference averaged
  about 0.36 seconds/frame on this CPU, versus 0.79 for the soccer detector.
- On thirteen manually selected visible people, the general detector covered
  thirteen targets and the soccer detector covered three. This is selected point
  coverage, not full recall, precision, or bounding-box mAP.
- Additional samples at 80:00 and 90:00 visually recover more players. People
  beside the pitch are also detected; no verified field polygon excludes them.
- Single-frame uniform checks: twelve correct and one incorrect on the selected
  thirteen targets. These checks isolate appearance from temporal tracking. Some
  references use these frames, so these are regression checks, not held-out
  accuracy estimates. The distant red goalkeeper in shadow remains a known miss.
- A shadowed goalkeeper reference was tested and removed from the active config:
  it made dark player kits less stable in longer sequences.
- Three ten-second sequences at 20:00, 40:00 and 60:00 were processed end to end.
  A five-second passage at 90:00 was also processed. Review outputs under
  `examples/soccer/output/person-comparison/` and `motion-comparison/`.

Repeat the detector comparison with `compare_detection.py --person_model`,
passing the source, output directory, four timestamps, and three frames per
sample. `evaluate_targets.py` evaluates marked points with one-to-one matching;
`evaluate_uniforms.py` checks known uniform labels on the same targets. Local
annotations are in `data/coverage-targets.json`.

## Camera compensation is experimental

`--compensate_camera_motion` excludes detected people from background optical
flow, estimates pan/zoom, and tracks in stabilized image coordinates. Original
image boxes and crops are retained. Failed estimates reset the tracking epoch
and classification history, avoiding reuse of invalid transforms and old IDs.

Replay matching complete source clips and saved detections using
`compare_tracking.py`. Both variants are replayed with the same current uniform
references, so changing references does not confound the comparison. The caller
must supply the exact clip that produced the saved detections; frame order and
count are checked, but old reports do not contain content hashes.

| Ten-second sample | Track IDs without/with compensation | Uniform changes without/with |
|---|---:|---:|
| 20:00 | 35 / 34 | 7 / 10 |
| 40:00 | 35 / 41 | 2 / 2 |
| 60:00 | 56 / 49 | 10 / 9 |

Fewer track IDs or label changes do not prove identity correctness. Results are
mixed, so compensation stays opt-in. Its coordinate system is an image reference
frame, not metres on the football pitch.

## Pitch calibration remains unverified

`inspect_calibration.py` produces confidence-labelled landmarks and a diagnostic
line overlay. On these samples the overlays do not follow the football markings;
even high model confidence did not establish correct correspondences. Do not
use these results for speed, distance, or trusted radar positions.

The camera is near halfway on the sideline and automatically follows play. Its
physical position is useful prior information, while pan and zoom change the
image mapping. The field also contains overlapping sets of markings. The
19:59–20:01 throw-in sequence supports the yellow touchline as the active boundary
in that view; one line is insufficient for a full homography. Review
`output/calibration-review/throw-in-boundary.jpg`.

The repository template assumes a 120m by 70m field with a 20.15m penalty-area
depth. The standard penalty-area depth is 16.5m ([IFAB Law 1](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-field-of-play/)).
Actual pitch dimensions and landmark identities need verification before metric
calibration. The original template has not been silently replaced.

## Validation and cleanup

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

Twenty-five checks cover person adaptation, tile merging, temporal classification,
point assignment, camera translation/zoom estimation, and tracking resets.
Earlier sampling outputs and redundant clips were removed at the user's request;
current clips, model weights and references remain. Generated data and reports
are ignored by Git.
